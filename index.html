The Lab

All-play truth · schedule luck · paper champions
🎯 The All-Play Universe — Every Week vs. Everyone

Head-to-head records are an accident of the schedule: you play one team a week, and who that is can matter as much as how you played. All-play erases the schedule entirely — every week, each team is scored against every other team at once. Win the week outright and you go 11–0; finish last and you go 0–11. Sum it up and you get the most objective measure of true strength there is: no exponent, no formula, no assumptions — just how often you'd have beaten the field. The gap between an owner's real wins and the wins their all-play rate earned is pure, empirical luck — the harder-nosed counterpart to the Pythagorean index below.

Carried vs. Robbed — True Strength against Real Record

Horizontal = all-play win% (true strength). Vertical = real win%. The dashed diagonal is where a record exactly matches performance. Sitting above the line means the schedule paid out; below it means the points were wasted. Regular season only · min. 3 seasons in range.

🎲 The Luck Index — Pythagorean Expectation

Points for and points against predict what a record should have been. The gap between an owner's actual wins and their Pythagorean expected wins is pure schedule luck — who their points happened to land against. Positive means the schedule smiled on them; negative means they got robbed.

Career Schedule Luck — Wins Above / Below Expected

🏆 Paper Champions vs. Real Champions

The luck index, taken to its logical end. Each season has a real champion — whoever lifted the trophy — and a paper champion: the owner who posted the league's highest Adjusted OPR that year, the team the numbers say was actually best. They almost never agree. Across league history the regular-season's best team went on to win it all just — proof that in this league, the title is won in the bracket, not on the spreadsheet.

The Two Trophy Cases — Rings Won vs. Rings Deserved

Gold = real championships won. Slate = paper titles (best Adjusted OPR in a season). Owners sorted by combined hardware. The 2011 title isn't shown here — that season's per-team OPR wasn't tracked.

The War Room

Skill positions · Min. 3 seasons to grade · K/DEF shown separately

There are no auction dollars in this league — the currency of a draft is the slot you spend. Every pick carries an expected return for where it was made; a player's actual production minus that expectation is the pick's surplus — the return on draft capital. Positive surplus is a steal, negative is a reach. Everything below is built on surplus, and kickers and defenses are kept in their own corner because their slot-value runs hot and tells you almost nothing.

📋 The Draft Capital Index — Who Drafts Best

Average surplus per skill-position pick, centered on the league average for the selected range. Zero is a perfectly average drafter; green beats the room, red trails it.

Avg. Draft Surplus per Skill Pick — vs. League Average

Surplus = a player's value over replacement minus the expected value of the slot they were drafted at. Min. 3 drafted seasons in range. K and DEF excluded.

🎯 Does Drafting Translate to Winning?

Each dot is an owner: how well they draft (across) against how often they win (up). The dashed line is the trend. Owners in the top-left win despite mediocre drafts — the waiver-and-trade artists. Owners in the bottom-right ace the draft and squander it.

The Wire & Trade Report

Did you win the deal? Every trade and waiver move from 2021–2025, graded two ways — raw points and value over replacement. Use the buttons to filter by season; every panel updates live.

How “winning” is scored. Each deal is graded two ways. Raw = total points each side’s incoming players scored from the trade onward. Value over replacement (VOR) = those same points measured against a freely-available player at the same position. A trade only counts as truly lopsided when both views agree; when they disagree, it lands in the Verdict Flips below.
